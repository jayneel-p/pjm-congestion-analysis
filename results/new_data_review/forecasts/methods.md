# Archived load vintages and a decision-time diagnostic

Written before calculations on 9 October 2026. This work reads canonical SQLite and writes only this directory. It uses existing D01/D02 measurement definitions, paired public prices A29/A32, load-forecast source A33/P24, and T02's information and risk discipline. No data are acquired and no source is newly saved.

The physical question is whether demand known through an earlier forecast helps distinguish the operating hours and named price exposure associated with A/B/C. The rival is that season and hour explain the same pattern, or that A's changed network/supply state overwhelms demand information. A useful falsifier is no improvement over a calendar-only benchmark on identical later hours. C's wind/export thesis cannot be tested as a pre-entry rule without historical renewable forecasts. Recent renewable forecasts are inventoried before use and excluded from the assessment if they do not overlap.

Keep all load forecast area/delivery/evaluation keys. Check duplicate and conflicting keys, numeric missingness, UTC/Eastern agreement, horizon and coverage. For each area and physical delivery hour select the latest evaluation no later than 09:00 Eastern on the preceding local calendar date. Preserve UTC and retain the evaluation, cutoff, evaluation age and forecast lead. Do not substitute a later vintage when no eligible vintage exists. Evaluation before the cutoff does not prove historical publication before the cutoff; label every output accordingly.

Use region-correct actual load pairs: RTO with PJM RTO Total, MIDATL with Mid-Atlantic Region, DOM with DOM Zone and COMED with COMED Zone. Do not substitute SOUTH/WEST, add overlapping footprints, or subtract regional renewable output from a local load forecast. Actual load uses the established supplied hour-ending minus one convention. Exclude the five documented clock-change dates for joins to supplied records. Public prices retain UTC/node keys. Anonymous gate clock/market remains provisional.

Report forecast error as actual minus forecast. Compare with the same actual entity seven days earlier, which precedes the cutoff. Each accuracy comparison uses common complete support, excluding the five clock-change dates in both the target delivery labels and the seven-day-lag source labels. An otherwise eligible target cannot use an ambiguous lag-source label. Save bias, MAE, RMSE, empirical 5/50/95-percentiles and large-error fractions. These test load accuracy under the supplied actual convention, not returns.

For gate/price models fix two chronological comparisons: train on 2024 through 24 December and evaluate 2025; train on 2024–2025 through 24 December and evaluate January–August 2026. This leaves a seven-day boundary gap. These periods were already explored: they are held-forward reconstructions, not a fresh confirmation sample. All forecasts for a delivery date use the same preceding-day cutoff. Fit on training data only. The calendar model uses month, hour and weekday/weekend categories plus month-by-hour and hour-by-week-type categories. The demand model adds separate cubic spline functions of the four forecast areas, with five knots fitted only on training data. Logistic regression predicts binding-hour occurrence; ridge regression predicts each exact named DA/RT total/congestion spread. Scale splines and use fixed regularization; do not tune on later outcomes. Calendar and demand models share every row and regularization. Save probabilities and prices, Brier/log loss, bias, MAE and RMSE, and ten equally populated reliability bins. A probability is not tradable odds until publication, clock, regime and calibration are validated.

Paired score uncertainty resamples whole calendar weeks 599 times. Compare demand minus calendar on the same resampled rows. This preserves within-week persistence but does not cover model selection, source uncertainty or future regime change. Also give year-specific occurrence and price distributions for high forecast-demand watch hours. Watch thresholds and calendar adjustments are estimated on the training set; compare each watch hour with same-month/hour/week-type nonwatch hours in the held-forward period. Require at least three controls. This is an explanatory watchlist diagnostic using realized later prices, not a calibrated strategy or matched causal effect. Retain tail hours and counterexamples. Use A's forecast RTO demand and C's MIDATL demand as narrow pressure screens; C's wind input remains missing before entry.

Prices are explicitly BGE_RESID_AGG minus WESTERN HUB (A), DOMINION HUB minus WESTERN HUB and DOM_RESID_AGG minus DOMINION HUB (B), and COMED_RESID_AGG minus WESTERN HUB (C), separately DA/RT and total/congestion. These proxies do not authenticate ICE contracts. The analysis has no executable quotes, official settlements, costs or profit claim.

Run the full rebuild in this order:

```sh
MPLCONFIGDIR=/private/tmp/pjm-mpl .venv/bin/python results/new_data_review/forecasts/analyze.py
MPLCONFIGDIR=/private/tmp/pjm-mpl .venv/bin/python results/new_data_review/forecasts/check_and_refine.py
MPLCONFIGDIR=/private/tmp/pjm-mpl .venv/bin/python results/new_data_review/forecasts/recent_benchmark.py
MPLCONFIGDIR=/private/tmp/pjm-mpl .venv/bin/python results/new_data_review/forecasts/refresh_accuracy.py --full-rebuild
```

`analyze.py` fits the models and writes the initial analysis. The next stages independently check point arithmetic, jointly resample watch targets and controls, add the delayed recent-history benchmark, then refresh common-support accuracy and final checks. The `--full-rebuild` flag skips comparison with old model/watch byte hashes because a complete refit may legitimately rewrite those files. For an accuracy-only refresh, omit the flag: the script then requires unchanged model/watch files against `pre_accuracy_fix_hashes.json`.

Independent point checks are recorded in `numeric_check.json`. Figures use saved tables; every plotted statistic has a retained count. Model intervals and empirical outcome quantiles are different objects.

The watchlist intervals jointly resample target and control weeks and recompute calendar-control means in each draw. When a sampled cell has no control weight, omit its targets for that draw and retain omitted-mass diagnostics. Fourteen-day clustering is a sensitivity. The CDF compares actual watch hours with individual control hours weighted by how many watch targets share their cell. It does not compare a volatile hourly distribution with the artificially smooth distribution of cell means. The plot shows the watch 1st–99th percentile window; every observation enters the CDF without renormalizing and omitted view mass is saved.

A second occurrence benchmark uses the trailing 28-day binding-hour fraction, ending seven days before the prior-day 09:00 cutoff. It assumes the anonymous gate history was available after that delay; its historical publication is also unverified. The rate updates through the evaluation period but never uses an outcome from the recent seven days or the delivery day. Compare it on the same model test rows. This tests whether recent regime information is more useful than the earlier-year calendar model; it is not a tradable calibrated forecaster. Do not choose among these benchmarks after observing a particular hour's outcome.

Accuracy-only refresh: `.venv/bin/python results/new_data_review/forecasts/refresh_accuracy.py` preserves the model, watchlist and recent-history files byte for byte, removes ambiguous lag-source hours from both accuracy arms, regenerates the accuracy graph and replaces the relevant numeric-audit rows. `final_hashes.json` records the refreshed artifact hashes.
